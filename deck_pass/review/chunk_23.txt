## regn
EXPL: "Regn" means "rain". To say it is raining, use the verb: "Det regner" (It is raining). Danish does not say "Det er regn".
SENT_DA: Der kommer meget **regn** i dag.
SENT_EN: A lot of rain is coming today.
RELATED: sne = snow; en byge = a shower; en paraply = an umbrella; en storm = a storm
## regnfuld
EXPL: "Regnfuld" means "rainy". It is built from "regn" (rain) and "-fuld" (full of), as in "fredfuld" (peaceful). To say a week was rainy: "Det har været en regnfuld uge" (It has been a rainy week).
SENT_DA: Det blev en **regnfuld** sommer, så vi var mest indendørs.
SENT_EN: It became a rainy summer, so we were mostly indoors.
RELATED: våd = wet; skyet = cloudy; tør = dry; solrig = sunny
## regnfuldt
EXPL: "Regnfuldt" means "rainy". It is built from "regn" (rain) and "-fuldt" (full of). To say it is raining right now, use the verb: "Det regner" (It is raining).
SENT_DA: Vejret har været **regnfuldt** hele ugen.
SENT_EN: The weather has been rainy all week.
RELATED: tør = dry; våd = wet; skyet = cloudy; solrig = sunny
## regntøj
EXPL: "Regntøj" means "rain gear". It is built from "regn" (rain) and "tøj" (clothes). This word has no article, so you say it without "et": "Tag regntøj på" (Put on rain gear).
SENT_DA: Børnene har **regntøj** på, fordi det regner.
SENT_EN: The children are wearing rain gear because it is raining.
RELATED: en regnjakke = a rain jacket; en paraply = an umbrella; overtøj = outerwear; en gummistøvle = a rubber boot
## regnvejr
EXPL: "Regnvejr" means "rainy weather". It is built from "regn" (rain) and "vejr" (weather). To say rain is falling right now, use the verb: "Det regner" (It is raining).
SENT_DA: Jeg tager en paraply med i **regnvejr**.
SENT_EN: I bring an umbrella in rainy weather.
RELATED: solskinsvejr = sunny weather; stormvejr = stormy weather; en regnbyge = a rain shower; en regnfrakke = a raincoat
## rejer
EXPL: "Rejer" means "shrimp". It is the plural of "en reje" (a shrimp). To say a sandwich with shrimp: "en rejemad" (a shrimp sandwich), built from "reje" and "mad" (food).

SENT_EN: I like eating fish and shrimp from the sea.
RELATED: skaldyr = shellfish; en krabbe = a crab; en hummer = a lobster; en musling = a mussel
## relativt

SENT_EN: The price is relatively low when you think about the quality.
RELATED: ganske = quite; forholdsvis = comparatively; nogenlunde = reasonably

## resten
EXPL: "Resten" means "the rest". To say you will do the remaining part later: "Jeg tager resten i morgen" (I will take the rest tomorrow). Do not mix it up with "rester" (leftovers): "Vi spiser rester" (We eat leftovers).
SENT_DA: Du kan få **resten** af kagen.
SENT_EN: You can have the rest of the cake.
RELATED: tilbage = left; det meste = most of it; en del = a part; alt = everything
## rester

SENT_EN: Today we eat leftovers from yesterday's dinner.

## retfærdig
EXPL: "Retfærdig" means "fair". The opposite is "uretfærdig" (unfair): "Det er uretfærdigt" (That is unfair). Danish also borrows the English word "fair": "Det er ikke fair" (That is not fair).
SENT_DA: Læreren er **retfærdig** og giver alle de samme chancer.
SENT_EN: The teacher is fair and gives everyone the same chances.
RELATED: rimelig = reasonable; lige = equal; upartisk = impartial; en retfærdighed = justice
## retfærdighed
EXPL: "En retfærdighed" means "justice". To talk about justice in general, leave out "en": "Der skal være retfærdighed" (There must be justice). The matching word for a decision is "retfærdig" (fair): "en retfærdig dom" (a fair verdict).
SENT_DA: **Retfærdighed** betyder, at alle behandles ens.
SENT_EN: Justice means that everyone is treated equally.
RELATED: en uretfærdighed = an injustice; en rettighed = a right (entitlement); en lov = a law
## retskrivning
EXPL: "En retskrivning" means "spelling rules". It is built from "ret" (correct) and "skrivning" (writing). In Denmark, the official spelling rules are in "Retskrivningsordbogen" (the spelling dictionary).
SENT_DA: Reglerne i dansk **retskrivning** hjælper os med at stave rigtigt.
SENT_EN: The rules of Danish spelling help us spell correctly.
RELATED: grammatik = grammar; tegnsætning = punctuation; ortografi = orthography; et komma = a comma
## retssikkerhed
EXPL: "En retssikkerhed" means "a legal certainty". It is built from "rets" (of the law) and "sikkerhed" (security). To say a reform makes people safer under the law: "Reformen styrker retssikkerheden" (The reform strengthens legal certainty).
SENT_DA: Uden **retssikkerhed** kan man blive straffet uden en fair rettergang.
SENT_EN: Without legal certainty, you can be punished without a fair trial.
RELATED: en rettighed = a right; retfærdighed = justice; en retsstat = a state under the rule of law; en domstol = a court

EXPL: "En risengrød" means "a rice porridge". It is built from "ris" (rice) and "en grød" (a porridge). The cold Christmas dessert made from it is "risalamande" (rice pudding with almonds): "Til jul får vi risalamande" (At Christmas we have risalamande).

## risikabel

## ro

SENT_DA: Børnene vil **sprøjte** vand på hinanden.
SENT_EN: The children want to spray water on each other.
RELATED: at stænke = to splash; at vande = to water; et sprøjtemiddel = a pesticide
## at spytte
EXPL: "At spytte" means "to spit". To say where you spit, add "på" (on): "spytte på gulvet" (spit on the floor). To say a dragon breathes out fire, use it with the fire: "Dragen spytter ild" (The dragon spits fire).
SENT_DA: Barnet kan ikke lide smagen og vil **spytte** den ud.
SENT_EN: The child does not like the taste and wants to spit it out.
RELATED: at synke = to swallow; at spy = to throw up; at sprøjte = to squirt
## at spænde
EXPL: "At spænde" means "to tighten or fasten". To tell someone to pull a strap tight, say: "Spænd remmen" (Tighten the strap). To say you tense your body, use it with the muscles: "Jeg spænder musklerne" (I tense my muscles).
SENT_DA: Du skal altid **spænde** sikkerhedsbæltet i bilen.
SENT_EN: You must always fasten the seat belt in the car.
RELATED: en spænding = tension / excitement; spændende = exciting; at løsne = to loosen; en gesp = a buckle
## at spøge
EXPL: "At spøge" means "to joke". To say you are not serious, add "kun" (only): "Jeg spøger kun" (I am only joking). To say a ghost is in a place, use "det" (it) and the place: "Det spøger i huset" (The house is haunted).
SENT_DA: Han kan godt **spøge** med sin egen fejl, og alle griner.
SENT_EN: He can joke about his own mistake, and everyone laughs.
RELATED: sjov = funny; at drille = to tease; alvorlig = serious; at more sig = to have fun
## at spørge
EXPL: "At spørge" means "to ask". To ask a person about something, add "om" (about): "Jeg spørger ham om vejen" (I ask him about the way). To ask for something you want, use "at bede om" (to ask for) instead: "Jeg beder om en kop kaffe" (I ask for a cup of coffee).
SENT_DA: Jeg vil **spørge** læreren.
SENT_EN: I want to ask the teacher.
RELATED: et spørgsmål = a question; at svare = to answer; at forhøre sig = to inquire
## at spørge om
EXPL: "At spørge om" means "to ask about". To ask about a thing, put it after "om" (about): "spørge om prisen" (ask about the price). To ask for something you need, use it the same way: "spørge om hjælp" (ask for help).
SENT_DA: Jeg vil **spørge om** hjælp.
SENT_EN: I want to ask for help.
RELATED: at svare = to answer; et spørgsmål = a question; at bede om = to ask for / request
## at spørge om vej

SENT_EN: I want to ask for directions to the station.
RELATED: at finde vej = to find one's way; at fare vild = to get lost; et kort = a map; at vise vej = to show the way

## at starte
EXPL: "At starte" means "to start". To say an event begins, use it with the time: "Vi starter i morgen" (We start tomorrow). To say a machine will not run, use it with the machine: "Bilen starter ikke" (The car will not start). To say a lesson begins, "at begynde" (to begin) works too: "Timen begynder klokken ni" (The lesson begins at nine).
SENT_DA: Filmen skal **starte** klokken otte.
SENT_EN: The movie is going to start at eight.
RELATED: at stoppe = to stop; at slutte = to finish; at fortsætte = to continue
## at starte på
EXPL: "At starte på" means "to start on". To say you begin a task, put "på" (on) before the task: "Jeg starter på opgaven" (I start on the assignment). To say an engine or a machine starts, use "at starte" (to start) without "på": "Bilen starter ikke" (The car does not start).
SENT_DA: Jeg vil **starte på** et nyt projekt i dag.
SENT_EN: I want to start on a new project today.
RELATED: at begynde på = to begin on; at afslutte = to finish; at afbryde = to interrupt; at stoppe = to stop

## at stemme

## at stige af
EXPL: "At stige af" means "to get off". To say you leave a bus, a train or a bike, put "af" (off) after the verb: "Hun stiger af cyklen" (She gets off the bike). To say the opposite, use "at stige på" (to get on): "Jeg stiger på bussen" (I get on the bus).
SENT_DA: Jeg vil **stige af** bussen ved torvet.
SENT_EN: I will get off the bus at the square.
RELATED: at stå af = to get off; at stige ud = to get out; at stige ind = to get in
