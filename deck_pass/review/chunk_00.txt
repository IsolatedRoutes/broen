## absolut
EXPL: "Absolut" means "absolutely". When you want to say "definitely not", put it before "ikke" (not): "absolut ikke" (absolutely not). When you want to say "definitely yes", use it alone: "Kommer du?" "Absolut!" (Are you coming? Absolutely!)
SENT_DA: Det er **absolut** umuligt at nå toget nu.
SENT_EN: It is absolutely impossible to catch the train now.
RELATED: bestemt = definitely; helt = completely; afgjort = definitely; sikkert = surely
## abstrakt
EXPL: "Abstrakt" means "abstract". When you name a style of art, put it before "kunst": "abstrakt kunst" (abstract art).
SENT_DA: Frihed er et **abstrakt** begreb.
SENT_EN: Freedom is an abstract concept.
RELATED: konkret = concrete; teoretisk = theoretical; uhåndgribelig = intangible
## absurd

RELATED: latterlig = ridiculous; meningsløs = pointless; vanvittig = insane
## adfærd
EXPL: "En adfærd" means "a behavior". Do not mix it up with "en handling" (an action). "Adfærd" is how someone acts in general: "Hundens adfærd er rolig" (The dog's behavior is calm). "En handling" is one act: "Det var en modig handling" (That was a brave act).
SENT_DA: Barnets **adfærd** i skolen er blevet bedre.
SENT_EN: The child's behavior at school has improved.
RELATED: at opføre sig = to behave; en opførsel = conduct; en vane = a habit

## adskillige

SENT_DA: Der kom **adskillige** gæster til festen.

RELATED: flere = more; mange = many; nogle = some; få = few

## afgjort
EXPL: "Afgjort" means "settled". It comes from the verb "at afgøre" (to decide), so it says that something is decided and certain. Put it before an adjective to mean "definitely": "Den nye bil er afgjort billigere" (The new car is definitely cheaper).

## afgørende
EXPL: "Afgørende" means "decisive". Do not mix it up with "vigtig" (important). Something "vigtig" matters: "Det er vigtigt at sove" (It is important to sleep). Something "afgørende" decides the result: "et afgørende møde" (a decisive meeting).
SENT_DA: Det sidste mål var **afgørende** for, hvem der vandt.
SENT_EN: The last goal was decisive for who won.
RELATED: at afgøre = to decide; væsentlig = significant; kritisk = critical
## afhængig
EXPL: "Afhængig" means "dependent". When you say what you depend on, add "af" (on): "Barnet er afhængigt af sine forældre" (The child is dependent on its parents). When you mean a habit you cannot stop, it also means "addicted": "Jeg er afhængig af kaffe" (I am addicted to coffee).

SENT_EN: My little brother is addicted to his phone.
RELATED: uafhængig = independent; en afhængighed = a dependence; selvstændig = self-reliant; at afhænge af = to depend on
## afhængig af
EXPL: "Afhængig af" means "dependent on". It is the adjective "afhængig" (dependent) followed by "af" (on). When you mean a habit you cannot stop, it also means "addicted to": "Han er afhængig af nikotin" (He is addicted to nicotine). When you say that a thing depends on something, use the verb "at afhænge af" (to depend on): "Det afhænger af prisen" (It depends on the price).
SENT_DA: En baby er **afhængig af** sine forældre.
SENT_EN: A baby is dependent on its parents.
RELATED: uafhængig = independent; en afhængighed = a dependence; selvstændig = self-reliant; at stole på = to rely on

## anden
EXPL: "Anden" means "second". When you count in order (first, second, third) and the word is an en-word, use "anden": "min anden dag" (my second day).
