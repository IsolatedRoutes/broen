## derhen
EXPL: "Derhen" means "there". To say you go toward a place, use it after the verb: "Vi cykler derhen" (We cycle there). To name the place where a person stays, use "der" (there): "Han er der" (He is there).
SENT_DA: Skal vi gå **derhen** sammen?
SENT_EN: Shall we walk there together?
RELATED: herhen = (to) here; derfra = from there; derover = (to) over there; derind = (to) in there
## derhjemme
EXPL: "Derhjemme" means "at home". To talk about home when you are somewhere else: "Hvad spiser I derhjemme?" (What do you eat at home?). To say you are in your house right now, use "hjemme" (at home): "Jeg er hjemme" (I am at home).
SENT_DA: **Derhjemme** spiser vi sent.
SENT_EN: At home we eat late.
RELATED: at tage hjem = to go home; herhjemme = at home (here); hjemmefra = from home
## derimod
EXPL: "Derimod" means "on the other hand". It shows a contrast with what was just said. To put the contrast after the verb: "Min bror er høj. Jeg er derimod lav." (My brother is tall. I, on the other hand, am short.) To put it right after the person: "Min søster derimod er lav" (My sister, on the other hand, is short).
SENT_DA: Byen er larmende. Landet er **derimod** stille.
SENT_EN: The city is noisy. The countryside, on the other hand, is quiet.
RELATED: men = but; til gengæld = in return; imidlertid = however; tværtimod = on the contrary
## derinde
EXPL: "Derinde" means "in there". It is built from "der" (there) and "inde" (inside). To say where someone is in another room: "Katten er derinde" (The cat is in there). For the room you are in, use "herinde" (in here): "Jeg er herinde" (I am in here).
SENT_DA: Min mor er **derinde** i stuen.
SENT_EN: My mother is in there in the living room.
RELATED: derude = out there; indenfor = inside; deroppe = up there
## dermed
EXPL: "Dermed" means "with that". It is built from "der" (there) and "med" (with). To say what follows from something you just did: "Han skrev under, og dermed er aftalen indgået" (He signed, and with that the deal is made).
SENT_DA: Vi har betalt, og **dermed** er sagen afgjort.
SENT_EN: We have paid, and with that the matter is settled.
RELATED: derfor = that is why; hermed = herewith / with this; således = thus
## dernede
EXPL: "Dernede" means "down there". To say where something is in a lower place: "Katten er dernede" (The cat is down there). For a higher place, use "deroppe" (up there): "Katten er deroppe" (The cat is up there).
SENT_DA: Bilen står **dernede** i kælderen.
SENT_EN: The car is down there in the basement.
RELATED: hernede = down here; derovre = over there; derinde = in there
## dernæst

SENT_DA: Først skærer du løget, og **dernæst** steger du det i smør.
SENT_EN: First you cut the onion, and then you fry it in butter.
RELATED: derefter = afterwards; til sidst = at the end; endelig = finally; senere = later
## deroppe
EXPL: "Deroppe" means "up there". To say where something is in a higher place: "Vores lejlighed ligger deroppe" (Our apartment is up there). For the place where you are, use "heroppe" (up here): "Heroppe er der koldt" (It is cold up here).
SENT_DA: Kan du se fuglen **deroppe** i træet?

RELATED: derovre = over there; derude = out there; dernede = down there
## derovre
EXPL: "Derovre" means "over there". To point to a place at a distance: "Bogen ligger derovre" (The book is over there). To say you go there, use "derover" (over there, with movement): "Jeg går derover" (I am going over there).

## derude

## det
EXPL: "Det" means "it". It stands for a thing that takes "et": "Jeg har et hus, og det er stort" (I have a house, and it is big). Things that take "en" use "den": "Jeg har en bil, og den er stor" (I have a car, and it is big). When you point to something just said or done, it means "that": "Det er sjovt" (That is fun). When there is no particular thing, such as weather: "Det regner" (It is raining).
SENT_DA: Se på huset! **Det** er meget gammelt.
SENT_EN: Look at the house! It is very old.
RELATED: de = they; dette = this (et-word); det der = that one
## det er
EXPL: "Det er" means "it is". To name or describe a thing: "Det er en bog" (It is a book). To point at someone and say who they are, it means "that is": "Det er min bror" (That is my brother).
SENT_DA: **Det er** en god dag i dag.
SENT_EN: It is a good day today.
RELATED: der er = there is; det var = it was; her er = here is
## det er en god idé
EXPL: "Det er en god idé" means "That's a good idea". To agree with a suggestion: "Skal vi gå en tur?" "Det er en god idé." (Shall we go for a walk? That's a good idea.) The short form is "God idé!" (Good idea!).
SENT_DA: Café i dag? **Det er en god idé.**
SENT_EN: Cafe today? That's a good idea.
RELATED: et forslag = a suggestion; det lyder godt = that sounds good; at foreslå = to suggest
## det er fint med mig

## det er i orden

## det er lige det
EXPL: "Det er lige det" means "Yes, that's exactly it". To confirm that someone has understood you: "Du mener, at vi skal vente?" "Det er lige det." (You mean we should wait? That's exactly it.) Said short, "lige det" means "exactly that": "Jeg ville have lige det" (I wanted exactly that).

## din
EXPL: "Din" means "your". It goes with a word that takes "en", such as "en hat" (a hat): "din hat" (your hat). With a word that takes "et", say "dit": "dit værelse" (your room). Before more than one thing, say "dine": "dine nøgler" (your keys).
SENT_DA: Her er **din** kaffe.
SENT_EN: Here is your coffee.
RELATED: min = my (en-word); jeres = your (group of people); dig = you (after the action); hans = his
