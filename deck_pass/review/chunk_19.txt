## naturlig
EXPL: "Naturlig" means "natural". To say something is normal and expected, add "t" after "det": "Det er helt naturligt at være nervøs" (It is completely natural to be nervous). To say something comes from the world as it is, not made by people, use it before a plural noun: "naturlige materialer" (natural materials).
SENT_DA: Hun har en **naturlig** evne til at tegne.
SENT_EN: She has a natural ability to draw.
RELATED: unaturlig = unnatural; kunstig = artificial; naturligvis = of course
## naturligvis
EXPL: "Naturligvis" means "of course". The ending "-vis" is also in "heldigvis" (luckily). To answer a question with yes, say it as a reply: "Kan du hjælpe?" "Naturligvis!" (Can you help? Of course!)
SENT_DA: **Naturligvis** hjælper jeg dig i morgen.
SENT_EN: Of course I will help you tomorrow.
RELATED: selvfølgelig = of course; klart = sure; bestemt = definitely; helt sikkert = for sure
## navnemåde
EXPL: "En navnemåde" means "an infinitive". It is built from "et navn" (a name) and "en måde" (a way), because this form only names the action. In Danish it has "at" (to) in front: "at spise" (to eat).
SENT_DA: Verbet at gå står i **navnemåde**.
SENT_EN: The verb at gå is in its plain form.
RELATED: en datid = a past tense; en bydemåde = an imperative; en nutid = a present tense
## navnlig
EXPL: "Navnlig" means "especially". It is built from "navn" (name) and the ending "-lig". To point out one example from a group, say: "Jeg kan lide sport, navnlig fodbold" (I like sports, especially soccer).
SENT_DA: Mange kan lide frugt, **navnlig** æbler.
SENT_EN: Many people like fruit, especially apples.
RELATED: især = above all; specielt = specifically; særligt = particularly; ikke mindst = not least
## ned
EXPL: "Ned" means "down". To tell someone to move to a lower place, say: "Kom ned!" (Come down!) To say where a person already is down there, use "nede" (down there): "Han er nede i kælderen" (He is down in the cellar).
SENT_DA: Elevatoren går op og **ned**.
SENT_EN: The elevator goes up and down.
RELATED: op = up; nedad = downward; nedenunder = downstairs
## nedad

SENT_EN: The water runs downward because the road slopes.

## nedenunder

SENT_DA: Vores nabo bor **nedenunder**.
SENT_EN: Our neighbor lives downstairs.
RELATED: ovenover = above; nedenfor = further below; oppe = up there
## nedslående

SENT_DA: Resultatet var **nedslående**, men vi giver ikke op.
SENT_EN: The result was disheartening, but we are not giving up.
RELATED: modløs = discouraged; skuffende = disappointing; opmuntrende = encouraging; deprimerende = depressing
## nedtrykt

## nej
EXPL: "Nej" means "no". When you are surprised or impressed, it also means "oh!": "Nej, hvor er det flot!" (Oh, how beautiful!) To answer yes to a question with "ikke" (not), say "jo" (yes) instead of "ja": "Kan du ikke komme?" "Jo, det kan jeg." (Can't you come? Yes, I can.)

## nem
EXPL: "Nem" means "easy". To say something is easy to do, use "nem at" (easy to) before the verb: "Appen er nem at bruge" (The app is easy to use). To say a task is hard instead, use "svær" (hard): "en svær opgave" (a hard task).
SENT_DA: En **nem** opskrift tager kun ti minutter.
SENT_EN: An easy recipe takes only ten minutes.
RELATED: besværlig = troublesome; let = light / easy; enkel = simple
## nemlig
EXPL: "Nemlig" means "exactly". To agree with someone, say it as a reply: "Er du træt?" "Nemlig!" (Are you tired? Exactly!) When you give a reason inside a sentence, it means "you see": "Jeg bliver hjemme, jeg er nemlig syg" (I am staying home, I am sick, you see).
SENT_DA: Jeg kan ikke komme, jeg er **nemlig** syg.
SENT_EN: I cannot come, I am sick, you see.
RELATED: præcis = precisely; lige præcis = exactly; jo = you know; faktisk = actually
## nemt
EXPL: "Nemt" means "easy". To say how something is done, it means "easily": "Han vinder nemt" (He wins easily). With an "en" word, use "nem" instead: "en nem opgave" (an easy task).
SENT_DA: Det er **nemt**: to plus to er fire.
SENT_EN: It is easy: two plus two is four.
RELATED: let = easy / light; svær = hard; enkelt = simply
## nervepirrende
EXPL: "Nervepirrende" means "nerve-wracking". It is built from "en nerve" (a nerve) and "pirrende" (irritating). To describe a movie that makes you tense, say: "en nervepirrende film" (a nerve-wracking movie).

SENT_EN: The wait before the exam result was nerve-wracking, and I slept badly.

## nervøs

## nervøsitet

SENT_DA: Man kan se hendes **nervøsitet**, når hun skal tale.
SENT_EN: You can see her nervousness when she has to speak.
RELATED: bekymret = worried; en angst = a fear; en uro = an unease; rolig = calm
## netop
EXPL: "Netop" means "exactly". To agree with exactly what someone said, say it as a reply: "Netop!" (Exactly!) To say something happened a moment ago, it means "just": "Han er netop gået" (He just left).
SENT_DA: Jeg skulle **netop** til at ringe til dig.

## neutral

## ni
EXPL: "Ni" means "nine". The same "ni" starts "nitten" (nineteen) and "niende" (ninth). To say a multiplication, say: "Tre gange ni er syvogtyve" (Three times nine is twenty-seven).
SENT_DA: Efter otte kommer **ni**.
SENT_EN: After eight comes nine.
RELATED: otte = eight; ti = ten; elleve = eleven
## niende

SENT_DA: Efter den ottende dag kommer den **niende** dag.
SENT_EN: After the eighth day comes the ninth day.
RELATED: ottende = eighth; tiende = tenth; første = first
## nitten

## nogen
EXPL: "Nogen" means "anyone". To say you see a person, it means "someone": "Jeg ser nogen" (I see someone). In a question, or after "ikke" (not), it also means "any": "Har du nogen børn?" (Do you have any children?)
SENT_DA: Der er ikke **nogen** hjemme.
SENT_EN: Nobody is home.
RELATED: ingen = no one; nogle = some; alle = everyone; noget = something
## nogenlunde

SENT_EN: I speak Danish reasonably well, but I still make mistakes.

## nogensinde
EXPL: "Nogensinde" means "ever". To ask about your whole life, use it in a question: "Har du nogensinde været i Paris?" (Have you ever been to Paris?) To say never, use "aldrig" (never): "Jeg har aldrig været i Paris" (I have never been to Paris).
SENT_DA: Har du **nogensinde** været i Norge?
SENT_EN: Have you ever been to Norway?
RELATED: altid = always; engang = once; ofte = often; sjældent = rarely
## noget
EXPL: "Noget" means "something". To say you see a thing, say: "Jeg ser noget" (I see something). To say you see a person, use "nogen" (someone): "Jeg ser nogen" (I see someone). Before something you cannot count one by one, such as milk, it means "some": "noget mælk" (some milk).
SENT_DA: Jeg er tørstig. Har du **noget** at drikke?
SENT_EN: I am thirsty. Do you have something to drink?
RELATED: ingenting = nothing; nogle = some; alt = everything
## nogle
EXPL: "Nogle" means "some", and it goes with more than one thing: "Jeg har nogle venner" (I have some friends). To talk about one thing, use "en" (a): "Jeg har en ven" (I have one friend). In a question, or after "ikke" (not), use "nogen" (any): "Har du nogen venner?" (Do you have any friends?)
SENT_DA: Der er **nogle** æbler på bordet.
SENT_EN: There are some apples on the table.
RELATED: noget = something; ingen = none; mange = many
## nogle gange
EXPL: "Nogle gange" means "sometimes" (literally, "some times"). To say you do something now and then, put it in the sentence: "Jeg spiser nogle gange morgenmad" (I sometimes eat breakfast). To say always, use "altid" (always): "Jeg spiser altid morgenmad" (I always eat breakfast).
SENT_DA: **Nogle gange** tager jeg bussen.
SENT_EN: Sometimes I take the bus.
RELATED: af og til = now and then; ofte = often; sjældent = rarely; som regel = usually
## nok

SENT_EN: No thanks, I have enough food.

## nord

SENT_DA: Du skal **opfylde** alle kravene, før du kan søge.
SENT_EN: You must fulfill all the requirements before you can apply.
RELATED: et krav = a requirement; at overholde = to comply with; at indfri = to redeem; at svigte = to let down
## at opføre
EXPL: "At opføre" means "to build or perform". To say a building is put up, say: "Kommunen opfører en ny skole" (The council is building a new school). To say a play is put on, say: "Teatret opfører et stykke" (The theater is putting on a play). With "sig" (oneself), it means "to behave": "at opføre sig pænt" (to behave nicely).
SENT_DA: Teatret vil **opføre** et stykke af Holberg i efteråret.
SENT_EN: The theater will stage a play by Holberg in the fall.
RELATED: at rive ned = to tear down; en opførelse = a construction; at konstruere = to construct; at iscenesætte = to stage
## at opføre sig
EXPL: "At opføre sig" means "to behave". To say how someone behaves, add a word like "pænt" (nicely) or "dårligt" (badly): "Han opfører sig dårligt" (He behaves badly). Without "sig" (oneself), "at opføre" means "to build": "De opfører et hus" (They are building a house).
SENT_DA: Børn skal **opføre sig** pænt i skolen.
SENT_EN: Children must behave nicely at school.
RELATED: en opførsel = a behavior; uartig = naughty; artig = well-behaved; høflig = polite
