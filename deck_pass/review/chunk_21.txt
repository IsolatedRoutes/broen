## en pastor
EXPL: "En pastor" means "a pastor". When you name a pastor, put "pastor" before the name: "Pastor Hansen holder gudstjeneste" (Pastor Hansen leads the service). The common word for a priest of the Danish church is "en præst" (a priest): "Præsten døber barnet" (The priest baptizes the child).
SENT_DA: Vores **pastor** holder gudstjeneste hver søndag klokken ti.
SENT_EN: Our pastor holds a service every Sunday at ten o'clock.
RELATED: en biskop = a bishop; en provst = a dean; en menighed = a congregation; en kirke = a church
## en patient
EXPL: "En patient" means "a patient", as in a person who receives medical care. The English word "patient" meaning calm is "tålmodig" in Danish: "Han er tålmodig" (He is patient).
SENT_DA: Lægen hjælper en syg **patient** på sygehuset.
SENT_EN: The doctor helps a sick patient at the hospital.
RELATED: en læge = a doctor; en sygeplejerske = a nurse; en behandling = a treatment

EXPL: "Primært" means "mainly". To say what a shop mostly sells, put it before the goods: "Vi sælger primært brugte bøger" (We mainly sell used books). To describe a noun, use "primær" (primary) instead: "det primære mål" (the primary goal).
SENT_DA: Butikken sælger **primært** brugt tøj, men også lidt legetøj.
SENT_EN: The store sells mostly used clothes, but also a few toys.

## på
EXPL: "På" means "on". To say where you are at a workplace, Danish uses it for "at": "Jeg er på arbejde" (I am at work). To name a language, it is used for "in": "Jeg skriver på dansk" (I write in Danish). To say you are on an island, it is used for "on": "Vi bor på Fyn" (We live on Funen).
SENT_DA: Telefonen ligger **på** bordet.
SENT_EN: The phone is on the table.
