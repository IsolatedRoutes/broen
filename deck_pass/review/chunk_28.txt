EXPL: "Tilgængelig" means "available". To ask if a person has time, say: "Er du tilgængelig?" (Are you available?). To say something can be found or used, say: "Bogen er tilgængelig online" (The book is available online). It also means "accessible": "Biblioteket er tilgængeligt for kørestolsbrugere" (The library is accessible for wheelchair users).
SENT_DA: Informationen er **tilgængelig** for alle på nettet.
SENT_EN: The information is available to everyone online.
RELATED: utilgængelig = unavailable; ledig = vacant; tilgængelighed = availability; til rådighed = at your disposal

## tilmed
EXPL: "Tilmed" means "moreover". To add one more good point, put it after "og" (and): "Maden er god, og tilmed er den billig" (The food is good, and moreover it is cheap). To say the same thing in other words, use "oven i købet" (on top of that): "Han er klog, og oven i købet er han flink" (He is smart, and on top of that he is kind).
SENT_DA: Cyklen er billig, og den er **tilmed** let at reparere.
SENT_EN: The bike is cheap, and it is also easy to repair.
RELATED: desuden = besides; endvidere = furthermore; derudover = in addition; også = also
## tilpas
EXPL: "Tilpas" means "just right". To say something is just right in degree, put it before an adjective: "Kaffen er tilpas stærk" (The coffee is just strong enough). To say you feel at ease, use "at føle sig" (to feel): "Jeg føler mig ikke tilpas" (I do not feel at ease).
SENT_DA: Vandet er **tilpas** varmt til at bade.
SENT_EN: The water is just warm enough for swimming.
RELATED: passende = suitable; behagelig = pleasant; utilpas = uneasy; nok = enough
## tilsammen

SENT_DA: Vi er ti personer **tilsammen**.
SENT_EN: We are ten people in total.

## tiltrækkende

## tirsdag
EXPL: "Tirsdag" means "Tuesday". In Danish, day names are not capitalized, unlike English "Tuesday". To say "on Tuesday" for the coming one, use "på tirsdag": "Vi ses på tirsdag" (See you on Tuesday). SENT_DA: Efter mandag kommer **tirsdag**.
SENT_EN: After Monday comes Tuesday.
RELATED: en mandag = a Monday; en onsdag = a Wednesday; en torsdag = a Thursday
## tja

## to
EXPL: "To" means "two". To count things, put it before the noun: "to børn" (two children), "to æg" (two eggs). In numbers like 22 the ones come first: "toogtyve" (twenty-two) is literally "two-and-twenty".
SENT_DA: En, **to**, tre!
SENT_EN: One, two, three!
RELATED: tre = three; dobbelt = double; begge = both; en = one
## to gange
EXPL: "To gange" means "twice". Literally, it is "two times". To say how often something happens, add "om dagen" (a day), "om ugen" (a week) or "om måneden" (a month): "to gange om ugen" (twice a week).
SENT_DA: Én gang, **to gange**, tre gange!
SENT_EN: Once, twice, three times!
RELATED: en gang = once; dobbelt = double; igen = again
