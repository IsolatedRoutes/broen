## identisk
EXPL: "Identisk" means "identical". To say two things are exactly the same, add "med" (with) and the other thing: "Svaret er identisk med mit" (The answer is identical to mine). The Danish word ends in "-isk", not "-ical".
SENT_DA: Hans kopi af nøglen er **identisk** med originalen.
SENT_EN: His copy of the key is identical to the original.
RELATED: ens = alike; forskellig = different; lignende = similar; uens = unlike each other
## idet
EXPL: "Idet" means "as". Literally, it is "i det" (in that), joined into one word. To say two things happen at the same moment, say: "Idet han gik ud, slukkede han lyset" (As he went out, he turned off the light).
SENT_DA: Han smilede, **idet** han åbnede døren.
SENT_EN: He smiled as he opened the door.
RELATED: mens = while; da = when; imens = meanwhile
## idræt
EXPL: "Idræt" means "PE". To say what class you have at school, say: "Vi har idræt efter frokost" (We have PE after lunch). Outside school, it means "sports" as an activity: "Hun elsker idræt" (She loves sports).
SENT_DA: Vi løber og spiller bold i **idræt**.
SENT_EN: We run and play ball in PE.
RELATED: en sport = a sport; gymnastik = gymnastics; en idrætshal = a sports hall; en idrætsmand = an athlete
## ifølge
EXPL: "Ifølge" means "according to". To name where the information comes from, say: "ifølge lægen" (according to the doctor). To give your own view instead, use "efter min mening" (in my opinion): "Efter min mening er filmen dårlig" (In my opinion the film is bad).
SENT_DA: **Ifølge** vejrudsigten bliver det koldt i morgen.
SENT_EN: According to the weather forecast, it will be cold tomorrow.
RELATED: en kilde = a source; angiveligt = allegedly; påstået = claimed; at hævde = to claim
## igen
EXPL: "Igen" means "again". To say you will return, say: "Jeg kommer igen" (I will come back). To say another person is included, use "også" (too): "Jeg kommer også" (I am coming too).

SENT_EN: Can you say that again?
RELATED: endnu en gang = once more; at gentage = to repeat; en gentagelse = a repetition
## igennem
EXPL: "Igennem" means "through". The shorter word "gennem" means the same: "gennem skoven" (through the forest). To say you read or check something from start to finish, put "igennem" after the verb: "Jeg læste bogen igennem" (I read the book all the way through).
SENT_DA: Vi går **igennem** parken.
SENT_EN: We walk through the park.
RELATED: forbi = past, by; hen over = across; at gennemgå = to go through
## ikke
EXPL: "Ikke" means "not". To say something is not so, put it after "er" (is): "Det er ikke dyrt" (It is not expensive). Do not mix it up with "nej" (no), which answers a question: "Nej, jeg er ikke sulten" (No, I am not hungry).
SENT_DA: Jeg er **ikke** sulten.
SENT_EN: I am not hungry.
RELATED: aldrig = never; ingenting = nothing; ingen = no / none
## ikke desto mindre
EXPL: "Ikke desto mindre" means "nevertheless". Literally, it is "not the less". To say something is still true despite what was just said, say: "Det var dyrt. Ikke desto mindre købte vi det" (It was expensive. Nevertheless, we bought it).
SENT_DA: Det regnede, men vi gik **ikke desto mindre** en tur.
SENT_EN: It rained, but we went for a walk nevertheless.
RELATED: trods alt = after all; dog = however; alligevel = anyway; på den anden side = on the other hand
## ikke endnu
EXPL: "Ikke endnu" means "not yet". To answer a question about being ready, say: "Er du klar?" "Ikke endnu." (Are you ready? Not yet.) To say you have not done something so far, put "endnu" (yet) at the end: "Jeg har ikke ringet endnu" (I have not called yet). Do not mix it up with "ikke mere" (not anymore), which says something has stopped: "Jeg bor ikke her mere" (I do not live here anymore).
SENT_DA: Har du spist? – **Ikke endnu**.
SENT_EN: Have you eaten? – Not yet.
RELATED: allerede = already; ikke længere = no longer; stadig = still
## ikke engang
EXPL: "Ikke engang" means "not even". To say something is missing although you would expect it, say: "Hun har ikke engang tid til en kop kaffe" (She does not even have time for a cup of coffee). The part "engang" also appears in the fairy-tale opening "Der var engang" (Once upon a time).

RELATED: endda = even; selv = even / self; overhovedet = at all
## ikke mere
