## hurra
EXPL: "Hurra" means "hooray". To cheer together, say "hip hip hurra" (hip hip hooray).
SENT_DA: **Hurra**, vi har fri i dag!
SENT_EN: Hooray, we have the day off today!
RELATED: juhu = yay; tillykke = congratulations; bravo = bravo; godt gået = well done
## hurtig
EXPL: "Hurtig" means "fast". To say how someone does an action, use "hurtigt" (fast) instead: "Han løber hurtigt" (He runs fast).
SENT_DA: Racerbilen er meget **hurtig**.
SENT_EN: The race car is very fast.
RELATED: langsom = slow; at skynde sig = to hurry; hastig = hasty; en hastighed = a speed
## husarbejde
EXPL: "Husarbejde" means "housework". It is built from "hus" (house) and "arbejde" (work). To say you have a new job, use "arbejde" alone: "Jeg har et nyt arbejde" (I have a new job).
SENT_DA: Jeg hader **husarbejde**, men jeg gør rent hver lørdag.
SENT_EN: I hate housework, but I clean every Saturday.
RELATED: rengøring = cleaning; at gøre rent = to clean; at rydde op = to tidy up
## husholdningsfilm
EXPL: "Husholdningsfilm" means "plastic wrap". It is built from "husholdning" (household) and "film". Here "film" means a thin sheet, not a movie.
SENT_DA: Jeg dækker skålen med **husholdningsfilm** og sætter den i køleskabet.
SENT_EN: I cover the bowl with plastic wrap and put it in the refrigerator.
RELATED: alufolie = aluminum foil; bagepapir = baking paper; en fryseboks = a freezer container; en plastikpose = a plastic bag
## husleje
EXPL: "Husleje" means "rent". It is built from "hus" (house) and "leje" (hire). To say you rent a car, use the verb "at leje" (to rent): "Jeg vil gerne leje en bil" (I would like to rent a car).
SENT_DA: Jeg betaler **husleje** den første hver måned.
SENT_EN: I pay rent on the first of every month.
RELATED: en udlejer = a landlord; en lejer = a tenant; et depositum = a deposit
## hvabehar
EXPL: "Hvabehar" means "pardon". It comes from "hvad behager", which literally means "what pleases". To show surprise, say it sharply: "Hvabehar! Er det sandt?" (What! Is it true?)
SENT_DA: **Hvabehar**? Kan du sige det igen?
SENT_EN: Pardon? Can you say it again?
RELATED: undskyld = excuse me, sorry; at gentage = to repeat; en gentagelse = a repetition
## hvad
EXPL: "Hvad" means "what". To ask about a thing, use "hvad": "Hvad er det?" (What is that?). To ask about a person, use "hvem" (who) instead: "Hvem er det?" (Who is that?). When you did not hear something, "Hvad?" (What?) is blunt. "Undskyld?" (Excuse me?) is more polite.
SENT_DA: **Hvad** hedder du?
SENT_EN: What is your name?
RELATED: hvilken = which; hvorfor = why; hvordan = how; hvor = where
## hvad betyder det?
EXPL: "Hvad betyder det?" means "What does that mean?". It is built from "hvad" (what), "betyder" (means) and "det" (that).
SENT_DA: Jeg forstår ikke ordet. **Hvad betyder det?**
SENT_EN: I don't understand the word. What does that mean?
RELATED: at betyde = to mean; en betydning = a meaning; at forklare = to explain; at forstå = to understand
## hvad er klokken?
EXPL: "Hvad er klokken?" means "What time is it?". Literally, it asks "what is the clock", because "klokken" means "the clock".
SENT_DA: Undskyld, **hvad er klokken?**
SENT_EN: Excuse me, what time is it?
RELATED: et ur = a watch / clock; et klokkeslæt = a time of day; et minut = a minute; et sekund = a second
## hvad hedder det på dansk?
EXPL: "Hvad hedder det på dansk?" means "What is that called in Danish?". The verb "hedder" (is called) is also used for names. To say your own name, say: "Jeg hedder Anna" (My name is Anna).
SENT_DA: Undskyld, **hvad hedder det på dansk?**
SENT_EN: Excuse me, what is that called in Danish?
RELATED: at hedde = to be called; et ord = a word; et navn = a name; hvad betyder det? = what does that mean?
## hvad hedder du?
EXPL: "Hvad hedder du?" means "What's your name?". Literally, it asks what you are called. The verb in it is "hedder" (are called).
SENT_DA: **Hvad hedder du?** - Jeg hedder Anna.
SENT_EN: What's your name? - My name is Anna.
RELATED: at hedde = to be called; et navn = a name; et fornavn = a first name; et efternavn = a last name
## hvad koster det?
EXPL: "Hvad koster det?" means "How much does it cost?". To ask about something that takes "et", such as "et hus" (a house), use "det" (it): "Hvad koster det?" To ask about something that takes "en", such as "en bog" (a book), use "den" (it): "Hvad koster den?" (How much does it cost?) To ask about several things, use "de" (they): "Hvad koster de?" (How much do they cost?)
SENT_DA: **Hvad koster det?** Jeg vil gerne købe det.
SENT_EN: How much does it cost? I'd like to buy it.
RELATED: at koste = to cost; en pris = a price; billig = cheap; dyr = expensive
## hvad laver du?
EXPL: "Hvad laver du?" means "What are you doing?". To ask about someone's work, say "Hvad arbejder du med?" (What do you work with?). To ask several people, use "I" (you all): "Hvad laver I?" (What are you all doing?)
SENT_DA: **Hvad laver du** i aften?
SENT_EN: What are you doing tonight?
RELATED: at lave = to do, to make; hvad skal du lave? = what are you going to do?; hvad lavede du? = what did you do?
## hvad med dig?
EXPL: "Hvad med dig?" means "What about you?". Literally, it is "what with you". To ask about a person by name, put the name in place of "dig" (you): "Hvad med Anna?" (What about Anna?)
SENT_DA: Jeg har det godt. **Hvad med dig?**
SENT_EN: I'm doing well. What about you?
RELATED: og dig? = and you?; hvad med i morgen? = what about tomorrow?; hvad med mig? = what about me?
## hvad som helst
EXPL: "Hvad som helst" means "anything". To say any person, use "hvem som helst" (anyone) instead: "Hvem som helst kan komme" (Anyone can come).
SENT_DA: Jeg er sulten og spiser **hvad som helst**.
SENT_EN: I am hungry and will eat anything.
RELATED: hvor som helst = anywhere; når som helst = anytime; alt = everything; ingenting = nothing
## hvad så?
EXPL: "Hvad så?" means "What's up?". Literally, it is "what then", because "så" means "then" here. The same "så" means "so" in other places: "Jeg er så glad" (I am so happy).
SENT_DA: Hej Anna, **hvad så?**
SENT_EN: Hi Anna, what's up?
RELATED: hvordan går det? = how is it going?; hvad nyt? = what's new?; hvad sker der? = what's happening?
## hvede
EXPL: "Hvede" means "wheat". It also starts other words, such as "hvedebrød" (wheat bread) and "hvedemel" (wheat flour).
SENT_DA: Af **hvede** laver man mel, og af mel bager man brød.
SENT_EN: From wheat you make flour, and from flour you bake bread.
RELATED: byg = barley; havre = oats; rug = rye; majs = corn
## hvedemel
EXPL: "Hvedemel" means "wheat flour". It is built from "hvede" (wheat) and "mel" (flour). Another word that starts with "hvede" is "hvedebrød" (wheat bread).
SENT_DA: Jeg bager boller med **hvedemel** og vand.
SENT_EN: I bake buns with wheat flour and water.
RELATED: rugmel = rye flour; fuldkornsmel = whole-grain flour; havremel = oat flour; majsmel = corn flour
## hvem
EXPL: "Hvem" means "who". English has two forms, "who" and "whom", but Danish uses "hvem" for both. When it asks who does the action, say: "Hvem taler?" (Who is speaking?) When it asks who is spoken to, say: "Hvem taler du med?" (To whom are you speaking?) To ask about a thing, use "hvad" (what) instead: "Hvad spiser du?" (What are you eating?)
SENT_DA: **Hvem** er din lærer?
SENT_EN: Who is your teacher?
RELATED: hvilken = which; hvor = where; hvornår = when; hvorfor = why
## hvem som helst
EXPL: "Hvem som helst" means "anyone". To say anything, use "hvad som helst" (anything) instead: "Jeg spiser hvad som helst" (I eat anything).
SENT_DA: I dag kan **hvem som helst** lære at svømme.
SENT_EN: Today anyone can learn to swim.
RELATED: nogen = someone; alle = everyone; ingen = no one; enhver = anybody
## hver
EXPL: "Hver" means "every". Put it before a word that takes "en", such as "en dag" (a day): "hver dag" (every day). For a word that takes "et", use "hvert" instead: "hvert år" (every year).
SENT_DA: Jeg løber **hver** morgen.
SENT_EN: I run every morning.
RELATED: hver gang = every time; hver anden = every other; enhver = anybody; alle = all
## hver dag
EXPL: "Hver dag" means "every day". It is built from "hver" (every) and "dag" (day). To say every second day, put "anden" (other) between them: "hver anden dag" (every other day).
SENT_DA: Jeg drikker kaffe **hver dag**.
SENT_EN: I drink coffee every day.
RELATED: hver morgen = every morning; hver gang = every time; hver måned = every month; hver aften = every evening
## hverken
EXPL: "Hverken" means "neither". It is used with "eller" (nor) as a pair: "hverken A eller B" (neither A nor B). To say you have neither of two things, say: "Jeg har hverken tid eller penge" (I have neither time nor money).
SENT_DA: Jeg vil **hverken** have kaffe eller te.
SENT_EN: I want neither coffee nor tea.
RELATED: enten = either; ingen = no / none; ingenting = nothing; aldrig = never
## hverken…eller
EXPL: "Hverken…eller" means "neither…nor". To say you have neither of two things, put "hverken" before the first and "eller" before the second: "Jeg har hverken tid eller penge" (I have neither time nor money). Do not add "ikke" (not), because the sentence already says no.
SENT_DA: Kaffe eller te? **Hverken…eller**, tak. Jeg tager vand.
SENT_EN: Coffee or tea? Neither, thanks. I will have water.
RELATED: enten…eller = either…or; både…og = both…and; ingen = no one; intet = nothing
## hvert
EXPL: "Hvert" means "every". Put it before a word that takes "et", such as "et år" (a year): "hvert år" (every year). For a word that takes "en", use "hver" instead: "hver dag" (every day).
SENT_DA: Vi rejser **hvert** år til Sverige.
SENT_EN: We travel to Sweden every year.
RELATED: alle = all; hver anden = every other; hver gang = every time; ethvert = any / every
## hvid

## hvidløg

## hvilke
EXPL: "Hvilke" means "which". Put it before a word that names more than one thing: "hvilke skoler" (which schools). To ask about one thing that takes "en", use "hvilken": "hvilken bil" (which car). For one thing that takes "et", use "hvilket": "hvilket hus" (which house).
SENT_DA: **Hvilke** sko vil du have?
SENT_EN: Which shoes do you want?
RELATED: hvad = what; hvem = who; hvor mange = how many; hvorfor = why
## hvilken
EXPL: "Hvilken" means "which". Put it before a word that takes "en", such as "en bil" (a car): "Hvilken bil vil du have?" (Which car do you want?) ## hvilket

SENT_DA: **Hvilket** hus er dit?

RELATED: hvilke = which (more than one); hvad = what; hvem = who; hvor = where
## hvis
EXPL: "Hvis" means "if". To say a condition, put "hvis" before it: "Hvis jeg har tid, kommer jeg" (If I have time, I will come). Do not mix it up with "om" (whether): "Jeg ved ikke, om han kommer" (I do not know whether he is coming).
SENT_DA: Jeg bliver hjemme, **hvis** det regner.
SENT_EN: I stay home if it rains.
RELATED: når = when; medmindre = unless; selvom = even though; ellers = otherwise
## hvis jeg var dig
EXPL: "Hvis jeg var dig" means "if I were you". To give advice, start with it and then say what you would do: "Hvis jeg var dig, ville jeg ringe til ham" (If I were you, I would call him). The verb "var" (was) is used here where English says "were".
SENT_DA: **Hvis jeg var dig**, ville jeg blive hjemme.
SENT_EN: If I were you, I would stay home.
RELATED: et råd = advice; at foreslå = to suggest; at anbefale = to recommend; hvis det var mig = if it were me
## hvor
EXPL: "Hvor" means "where". To ask "how" about a quality or an amount, put "hvor" before the word: "hvor gammel" (how old), "hvor mange" (how many).
SENT_DA: **Hvor** er stationen?
SENT_EN: Where is the station?
RELATED: her = here; der = there; hvorfra = from where; hvordan = how
## hvor er toilettet?
EXPL: "Hvor er toilettet?" means "Where is the restroom?". It is built from "hvor" (where), "er" (is) and "toilettet" (the toilet), where the ending "-et" means "the".
SENT_DA: Undskyld, **hvor er toilettet?**
SENT_EN: Excuse me, where is the restroom?
RELATED: hvor er stationen? = where is the station?; et badeværelse = a bathroom; hvor er udgangen? = where is the exit?
## hvor gammel er du?
EXPL: "Hvor gammel er du?" means "How old are you?". It is built from "hvor" (how), "gammel" (old), "er" (are) and "du" (you). To say your age, you can leave out "gammel": "Jeg er tyve år" (I am twenty years old).
SENT_DA: Anna, **hvor gammel er du?**
SENT_EN: Anna, how old are you?
RELATED: ung = young; en alder = an age; hvor høj er du? = how tall are you?
## hvor kommer du fra?
EXPL: "Hvor kommer du fra?" means "Where are you from?". Literally, it asks where you come from. To answer, say: "Jeg kommer fra England" (I come from England). The word "fra" (from) stays at the end of the question, as in English.
SENT_DA: Hej, **hvor kommer du fra?**
SENT_EN: Hi, where are you from?
RELATED: hvor bor du? = where do you live?; hvor er du fra? = where are you from?; at komme = to come
## hvor meget koster det?
EXPL: "Hvor meget koster det?" means "How much does it cost?". Word by word, it is "how much costs it". To ask about weight, use the same start with another verb: "Hvor meget vejer det?" (How much does it weigh?)
SENT_DA: Jeg vil købe brødet. **Hvor meget koster det?**
SENT_EN: I want to buy the bread. How much does it cost?
RELATED: at koste = to cost; en pris = a price; hvad koster det? = what does it cost?
## hvor som helst
EXPL: "Hvor som helst" means "anywhere". The ending "som helst" also follows other question words: "hvem som helst" (anyone), "hvad som helst" (anything) and "hvornår som helst" (any time).
SENT_DA: Du kan sidde **hvor som helst** i rummet.
SENT_EN: You can sit anywhere in the room.
RELATED: overalt = everywhere; ingen steder = nowhere; et eller andet sted = somewhere
## hvordan
EXPL: "Hvordan" means "how". To ask how old someone is, use "hvor" (how) before the word instead: "Hvor gammel er du?" (How old are you?) To ask how many, do the same: "Hvor mange børn har du?" (How many children do you have?)
SENT_DA: **Hvordan** kommer jeg til stationen?
SENT_EN: How do I get to the station?
RELATED: hvorfor = why; hvad = what; hvornår = when
## hvordan går det?
EXPL: "Hvordan går det?" means "How's it going?". Literally, it is "how goes it". To ask the other person back after you answer, add "og dig?" (and you?): "Det går godt, og dig?" (It's going well, and you?)
SENT_DA: Hej Anna, **hvordan går det?**
SENT_EN: Hi Anna, how's it going?
RELATED: hvordan har du det? = how are you?; det går ok = it's going OK; hvad så? = what's up?
## hvordan har du det?
EXPL: "Hvordan har du det?" means "How are you?". Literally, it is "how have you it". To answer, say "Jeg har det godt" (I'm doing well) or "Fint, tak" (Fine, thanks).
SENT_DA: Hej Peter! **Hvordan har du det?**
SENT_EN: Hi Peter! How are you?
RELATED: hvad så? = what's up?; hvad nyt? = what's new?; alt vel? = all well?
## hvorefter
EXPL: "Hvorefter" means "after which". It is built from "hvor" (which) and "efter" (after). To join two events in a simpler way, use "og så" (and then): "Hun lukkede bogen, og så slukkede hun lyset" (She closed the book, and then she turned off the light).
SENT_DA: Hun lukkede bogen, **hvorefter** hun slukkede lyset og sov.
SENT_EN: She closed the book, after which she turned off the light and slept.
RELATED: bagefter = afterwards; efterfølgende = subsequently; senere = later; før = before
## hvorfor
EXPL: "Hvorfor" means "why". To give a reason, start your answer with "fordi" (because): "Hvorfor kommer du ikke?" "Fordi jeg er syg." (Why aren't you coming? Because I am sick.)
SENT_DA: **Hvorfor** er du så glad?
SENT_EN: Why are you so happy?
RELATED: derfor = therefore; på grund af = because of; en grund = a reason; hvornår = when
## hvorfra
EXPL: "Hvorfra" means "from where". It is built from "hvor" (where) and "fra" (from). The word "fra" (from) also ends the question "Hvor kommer du fra?" (Where do you come from?)
SENT_DA: **Hvorfra** kommer din ven?
SENT_EN: Where does your friend come from?
RELATED: hvordan = how; hvorfor = why; hvornår = when; derfra = from there
## hvorimod
EXPL: "Hvorimod" means "whereas". To contrast two statements, put it after a comma before the second one: "Jeg drikker kaffe, hvorimod hun drikker te" (I drink coffee, whereas she drinks tea). To say two things happen at the same time, use "mens" (while) instead: "Jeg læser, mens du sover" (I read while you sleep).
SENT_DA: Jeg bor i byen, **hvorimod** min søster bor på landet.
SENT_EN: I live in the city, whereas my sister lives in the country.
RELATED: men = but; derimod = by contrast; til gengæld = on the other hand; imens = meanwhile
## hvornår
EXPL: "Hvornår" means "when" in a question. To say "when" in the middle of a sentence about the future, use "når" (when): "Jeg ringer, når jeg kommer hjem" (I will call when I get home). To say "when" about one finished event in the past, use "da" (when): "Jeg var glad, da du kom" (I was happy when you came).
SENT_DA: **Hvornår** åbner butikken?
SENT_EN: When does the store open?
RELATED: hvordan = how; hvorfor = why; hvor = where
## hvorved
EXPL: "Hvorved" means "whereby". It is built from "hvor" (where) and "ved" (by). To introduce the result of an action, put it after a comma that follows the action: "Han trykkede på knappen, hvorved døren gik op" (He pressed the button, whereby the door opened).
SENT_DA: Hun skrev under, **hvorved** aftalen blev gyldig.
SENT_EN: She signed, whereby the agreement became valid.
RELATED: derved = thereby; herved = hereby; hvormed = with which; hvortil = to which
## hvorvidt

SENT_DA: Politiet undersøger, **hvorvidt** manden har sagt sandheden.
SENT_EN: The police are investigating whether the man has told the truth.

SENT_DA: Lægen giver hende en **indsprøjtning** mod smerterne i armen.
SENT_EN: The doctor gives her an injection against the pain in her arm.
RELATED: en vaccination = a vaccination; en injektion = an injection; en dosis = a dose; en kur = a course of treatment
